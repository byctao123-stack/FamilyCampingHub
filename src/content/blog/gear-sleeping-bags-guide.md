## A Good Night's Sleep Makes the Trip

There's a saying in camping: "There's no such thing as bad weather, only bad gear." A poor sleeping bag can turn a wonderful camping trip into a miserable experience. But the right sleeping bag ensures everyone wakes up rested and ready for adventure.

## Understanding Temperature Ratings

This is the most important (and most misunderstood) spec on a sleeping bag.

### What the Rating Means

The temperature rating indicates the lowest temperature at which the average sleeper will stay warm. For example:

- A **30°F bag** should keep you warm down to 30°F
- A **50°F bag** is for warm summer nights

### Rating Systems

| System | Description |
|--------|-------------|
| **EN/ISO Rating** | Standardized, most reliable |
| **Manufacturer Rating** | Often optimistic — subtract 10-15°F |
| **Comfort vs. Limit** | Comfort = cozy for average sleeper; Limit = survive for cold sleepers |

### Real-World Rule

Always choose a bag rated **10-15°F lower** than the coldest expected night temperature. If nighttime lows will be 40°F, get a 25-30°F bag.

## Types of Sleeping Bags

### Mummy Bags
- **Pros:** Best warmth-to-weight ratio, efficient
- **Cons:** Narrow fit can feel confining
- **Best for:** Backpackers, cold weather

### Rectangular Bags
- **Pros:** Roomy, can use as a blanket, double up
- **Cons:** Heavier, less efficient
- **Best for:** Car camping, warm weather

### Semi-Rectangular (Barrel) Bags
- **Pros:** Balance of room and warmth
- **Cons:** Middle ground — not the best at anything
- **Best for:** Three-season family camping

## Kids Sleeping Bags

Children need special consideration:

### Size
- Kids bags are shorter (saves weight and warmth)
- Look for bags sized for ages 3-6 or 7-12
- Some grow with your child (extra room in footbox)

### Temperature
- Kids sleep colder than adults
- Get a bag rated 10-15°F warmer than you think you need
- Consider a grow-with-me bag for young kids

### Fun Factor
- Let kids pick their color/pattern
- Look for bags with built-in pillows or pockets
- Consider sleeping bag liners for extra warmth

## Sleeping Bag Care

### First Use
- Air it out before first use
- Loft the insulation by shaking it out
- Wash if it has a factory smell

### During Trips
- Unroll as soon as you arrive
- Fluff and shake before getting in
- Keep it dry (use a sleeping bag liner in damp conditions)

### After Trips
- Hang to dry completely before storing
- Never store compressed — use a large cotton storage sack
- Wash annually with technical cleaner

## Essential Accessories

### Sleeping Bag Liners
- Adds 10-15°F of warmth
- Keeps the bag clean
- Soft and comfortable against skin
- Easy to wash

### Sleeping Pads
- **Foam pads:** Durable, cheap, bulky
- **Self-inflating:** Comfortable, medium price
- **Air pads:** Most comfortable, can leak
- **Pro tip:** The pad is often more important than the bag for warmth

### Pillows
- Inflatable camping pillows (compact)
- Stuff sack with clothes (DIY option)
- Memory foam travel pillows

## Winter Camping Considerations

If you camp in cold weather (below 30°F):

1. **Layer up:** Use a bag liner inside your bag
2. **Hot water bottle:** Fill a bottle with hot water and put in your bag
3. **Wear a hat:** You lose most heat through your head
4. **Don't breathe in the bag:** Moisture from breath reduces insulation
5. **Insulate from the ground:** A good pad is essential in cold weather

## Buying Guide Checklist

- [ ] Temperature rating appropriate for your climate
- [ ] Right shape (mummy vs. rectangular)
- [ ] Size fits (check length for growing kids)
- [ ] Material preference (synthetic vs. down)
- [ ] Machine washable (for kids' bags)
- [ ] Compression sack included
- [ ] Warranty information
