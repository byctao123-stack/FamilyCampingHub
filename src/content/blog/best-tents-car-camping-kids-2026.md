Car camping with kids is a different animal than backpacking or solo trips. You're not counting ounces — you're counting how many meltdowns you can prevent before bedtime. And nothing triggers a kid meltdown faster than "we still have to set up the tent" after a long drive.

That's why car camping tents need to check different boxes: **quick setup** (because tired kids don't wait), **real space** (because gear multiplies with children), and **weather protection** (because rain doesn't care about your plans).

I've been testing two of the most popular family car camping tents on Amazon: the **Coleman WeatherMaster 6P** with its signature screened porch, and the **CORE 9P Instant Cabin** that literally sets up in 2 minutes. They take very different approaches to the same problem, and after spending time with both, I've got strong opinions about which one fits which family.

Let's break it down.

**One-liner:** A cabin tent with a screened-in porch — the closest thing to a front porch in the outdoors.

### Why Families Love It

**1. The screened porch is a game-changer**

This is the WeatherMaster's headline feature, and it delivers. That 33 sq ft screened room is where you eat breakfast, play cards when it rains, or store muddy boots without tracking them into the sleeping area. For families with young kids, it's an extra "room" that keeps bugs out while letting the outdoors in.

One reviewer nailed it: *"The screen is a nice touch to sit on our chairs while drinking coffee."*

![Coleman WeatherMaster screened porch detail](/images/articles/best-tents-car-camping-kids-2026/04-coleman-weathermaster-setup.jpg)

**2. Standing room height**

At 6 ft 8 in the center, this is one of the tallest family tents in its class. You can stand up, change clothes, and help kids get dressed without crouching. When you're dealing with wiggly toddlers who refuse to hold still, standing room is not a luxury — it's a necessity.

**3. WeatherTec keeps you dry**

Coleman's WeatherTec system with welded corners and inverted seams is proven over decades. Multiple reviewers confirmed staying dry through rain and wind gusts up to 37 mph. One family waterproofed theirs with additional spray and reported zero leaks through 12 hours of heavy rain.

![Coleman WeatherMaster interior space](/images/articles/best-tents-car-camping-kids-2026/03-coleman-weathermaster-interior.jpg)

### What Could Be Better

- **Setup is not 20 minutes for everyone.** Coleman says 20 minutes; some reviewers found it longer, especially the first time. The color-coded poles help, but there are a lot of poles and sleeves to manage. This is a two-person job.
- **Leaky windows.** Multiple reviewers reported small amounts of water leaking around the windows in heavy rain. Adding waterproofing spray helps, but it shouldn't be necessary on a $360 tent.
- **Heavy at 37 lbs.** This is strictly a car camping tent. You're not carrying this far from the parking lot.
- **Only 38 reviews.** With a 3.8 rating from just 38 reviews, the track record is limited compared to the CORE's 7,000+ reviews.
- **Zipper and velcro issues.** Some reviewers noted the side door needs more velcro attachments to stay closed properly.

## The Verdict: Who Should Buy Which?

### 🏕️ Choose the Coleman WeatherMaster if...

- **A screened porch is non-negotiable.** If your family's idea of camping includes morning coffee without mosquitoes, card games without ants, or a dry place for kids to play when it rains — that porch is worth every penny of the $110 premium.
- **You camp with two adults who can handle setup.** The 20-minute, two-person setup is manageable if you're not doing it alone.
- **You value standing room.** At 6 ft 8 in, it's the tallest option here. If you or your partner are tall, you'll appreciate not hunching.
- **You want a "base camp" tent.** If you'll spend most of your time at the campsite (not just sleeping), the porch extends your living space significantly.

### ⚡ Choose the CORE 9P if...

- **Setup speed is everything.** Solo parent? Tired kids? arriving after dark? The 2-minute setup is a genuine quality-of-life upgrade.
- **You need maximum sleeping space.** 126 sq ft with two doors and a room divider — this tent handles families of 5-6 with gear.
- **You want proven reliability.** 7,115 reviews at 4.6 stars is as close to a guaranteed good purchase as you'll find in camping gear.
- **Budget matters.** $250 vs. $360 — that $110 savings goes a long way toward other gear.
- **You might set up solo.** The one-person setup capability is a real advantage for single parents or when your camping partner is wrangling kids.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q: Is the Coleman's screened porch worth $110 extra?

**A:** It depends on how you camp. If you spend lots of time at the campsite — cooking, eating, playing games, just hanging out — the porch is a fantastic addition. It's essentially a second room that keeps bugs out. If you're mostly sleeping at the tent and spending daytime elsewhere (hiking, swimming, exploring), you won't use it as much, and the CORE's extra interior space might serve you better.

### Q: Can the CORE 9P really sleep 9 people?

**A:** Technically yes, but realistically it's comfortable for 4-6 people with gear. The "9-person" rating assumes sleeping bags on the floor with no airbeds or equipment. For a family of 4-5 with airbeds and gear, the 126 sq ft is spacious and comfortable.

### Q: Which tent handles rain better?

**A:** Both handle moderate rain well. The Coleman's WeatherTec is a proven system, and the porch gives you a dry staging area. The CORE's H2O Block with 1200mm fabric has been battle-tested by thousands of reviewers, including some in extreme conditions. For heavy rain, I'd add a ground tarp with either tent. The screened porch gives the Coleman an edge for rainy-day comfort — you have somewhere dry to sit outside the sleeping area.

## The Bottom Line

These two tents solve the same problem — comfortable family car camping — but in fundamentally different ways.

The **Coleman WeatherMaster** is a *living* tent. That screened porch transforms it from a place to sleep into a place to hang out. It's slower to set up, heavier, and has fewer reviews, but if your family values outdoor lounging space, it's the only game in town.

The **CORE 9P** is a *sleeping* tent done extremely right. It sets up in 2 minutes, fits an absurd amount of people and gear, and has been validated by 7,000+ happy families. It doesn't have a porch, but what it does have — space, speed, and reliability — it does better than almost anything in its price range.

My take? If I were buying for my family, I'd go **CORE 9P** for the speed and value, and add a separate $50 screen house for the porch experience. Best of both worlds for less than the Coleman alone.

But if that integrated porch is calling your name — and for many families, it will — the WeatherMaster earns its premium.
